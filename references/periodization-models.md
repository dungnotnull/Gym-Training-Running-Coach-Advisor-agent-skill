# Periodization Models Implementation Guide

## Research Foundation

**Primary Sources:**
1. T. Bompa, C. Buzzichelli (2018). Periodization: Theory and Methodology of Training (6th ed.)
2. G. Zatsiorsky, W. Kraemer (2006). Science and Practice of Strength Training
3. M. Rhea et al. (2003). A Meta-Analysis to Determine the Dose Response for Strength Development

## Core Principle

Periodization is the systematic planning of athletic training to reach the best possible performance at the most important time. It involves progressive cycling of various aspects of a training program during a specific period.

## Periodization Model Selection Guide

### Choose Linear Periodization When:
- Athlete is beginner or intermediate
- Clear single competition/peaking date exists
- Long preparation period available (12+ weeks)
- Simplicity and predictability valued

### Choose Undulating/DUP When:
- Athlete is intermediate or advanced
- Multiple competitions or no fixed competition date
- Benefits from variety in training stimuli
- Can handle more complex programming

### Choose Block Periodization When:
- Athlete is advanced
- Multiple qualities need development sequentially
- Limited time to develop all qualities
- Competition schedule allows focused blocks

## Model 1: Linear Periodization

### Structure

**Mesocycle:** 8-12 weeks
**Progression:** High volume → Low volume; Low intensity → High intensity

### Typical Mesocycle Structure

**Phase 1: Hypertrophy/Endurance (Weeks 1-4)**
- Volume: High (3-4 sets, 10-15 reps)
- Intensity: Low (50-65% 1RM)
- Focus: Build work capacity, muscle mass

**Phase 2: Strength/Power (Weeks 5-8)**
- Volume: Moderate (3-4 sets, 5-8 reps)
- Intensity: Moderate (70-85% 1RM)
- Focus: Build strength foundation

**Phase 3: Peaking (Weeks 9-12)**
- Volume: Low (1-3 sets, 1-5 reps)
- Intensity: High (85-100% 1RM)
- Focus: Maximize strength/power

### Example: 12-Week Linear Strength Program

**Weeks 1-4 (Hypertrophy):**
- Squat: 4x10 @ 65% 1RM
- Bench: 4x10 @ 65% 1RM
- Deadlift: 3x8 @ 60% 1RM
- Press: 3x12 @ 60% 1RM

**Weeks 5-8 (Strength):**
- Squat: 4x6 @ 80% 1RM
- Bench: 4x6 @ 80% 1RM
- Deadlift: 3x5 @ 75% 1RM
- Press: 3x8 @ 75% 1RM

**Weeks 9-12 (Peak):**
- Squat: 3x3 @ 90% 1RM
- Bench: 3x3 @ 90% 1RM
- Deadlift: 2x3 @ 85% 1RM
- Press: 3x5 @ 85% 1RM

**Deload:** Every 4th week (reduce volume 50%)

### Advantages
- Simple to understand and execute
- Clear progression path
- Effective for beginners and intermediates
- Predictable adaptation pattern

### Disadvantages
- Can lead to boredom from repetition
- May not develop all qualities equally
- Peaking is narrow (short performance window)

## Model 2: Undulating Periodization (DUP)

### Structure

**Timeframe:** Weekly microcycles within longer macrocycle
**Pattern:** High/Medium/Low intensity sessions within each week

### Daily Undulating Periodization (DUP)

**Weekly Pattern (3x/week):**
- **Monday:** Heavy (3-5 reps, 85-95% 1RM)
- **Wednesday:** Medium (6-10 reps, 70-85% 1RM)
- **Friday:** Light (12-15 reps, 50-70% 1RM)

### Example: DUP Strength Program

**Week 1:**
- Monday (Heavy): Squat 4x5 @ 85%, Bench 4x5 @ 85%
- Wednesday (Medium): Squat 4x8 @ 70%, Bench 4x8 @ 70%
- Friday (Light): Squat 3x12 @ 60%, Bench 3x12 @ 60%

**Week 2 (progression):**
- Monday (Heavy): Squat 4x5 @ 87%, Bench 4x5 @ 87%
- Wednesday (Medium): Squat 4x8 @ 72%, Bench 4x8 @ 72%
- Friday (Light): Squat 3x12 @ 62%, Bench 3x12 @ 62%

**Deload:** Every 4th week (all sessions at 60% of normal)

### Advanced DUP Patterns

**4x/week Split:**
- Day 1: Heavy Squat + Light Bench
- Day 2: Light Deadlift + Heavy Press
- Day 3: Medium Squat + Medium Bench
- Day 4: Heavy Deadlift + Light Press

**6x/week (advanced):**
- Mon: Heavy Lower
- Tue: Light Upper
- Wed: Medium Lower
- Thu: Rest
- Fri: Heavy Upper
- Sat: Light Lower

### Advantages
- Variety reduces training boredom
- Frequent stimulation of all qualities
- Can delay adaptation plateau
- Flexible for scheduling conflicts

### Disadvantages
- More complex to program
- Harder to track progress across intensities
- Requires good training intuition
- Can be fatiguing if not managed well

## Model 3: Block Periodization

### Structure

**Block Duration:** 4-6 weeks per block
**Sequence:** Accumulation → Transmutation → Realization

### Block Types

**Block 1: Accumulation**
- Duration: 4-6 weeks
- Volume: High
- Intensity: Low to moderate
- Focus: Build work capacity, general preparation
- Exercises: High variety, general movements

**Block 2: Transmutation**
- Duration: 4-6 weeks
- Volume: Moderate
- Intensity: Moderate to high
- Focus: Convert general qualities to specific
- Exercises: More specific to sport/goal

**Block 3: Realization**
- Duration: 2-4 weeks
- Volume: Low
- Intensity: High
- Focus: Maximize specific performance
- Exercises: Highly specific, competition movements

### Example: 12-Week Block for Powerlifting

**Block 1 (Weeks 1-4): Accumulation**
- Squat: 4x8 @ 70%, 4x6 @ 75%
- Bench: 4x10 @ 65%, 4x8 @ 70%
- Deadlift: 4x6 @ 70%, 4x5 @ 75%
- Accessory: High volume, general strengthening

**Block 2 (Weeks 5-8): Transmutation**
- Squat: 5x5 @ 80%, 3x3 @ 85%
- Bench: 5x5 @ 80%, 3x3 @ 85%
- Deadlift: 4x4 @ 80%, 3x3 @ 85%
- Accessory: Reduced, more specific

**Block 3 (Weeks 9-12): Realization**
- Week 9: Squat 3x3 @ 90%, Bench 3x3 @ 90%, Deadlift 2x3 @ 85%
- Week 10: Squat 2x3 @ 92%, Bench 2x3 @ 92%, Deadlift 2x2 @ 90%
- Week 11: Squat 1x3 @ 95%, Bench 1x3 @ 95%, Deadlift 1x2 @ 95%
- Week 12: Competition or Testing Week

**Deload:** 1 week between blocks (reduce all training by 50%)

### Advanced Block Sequencing

**For athletes with multiple competitions:**

**Annual Structure:**
1. General Prep Block (8 weeks)
2. Specific Prep Block (6 weeks)
3. Competition 1
4. Recovery Block (2 weeks)
5. Specific Prep Block 2 (4 weeks)
6. Competition 2
7. Transition Block (4 weeks)

### Advantages
- Highly focused development
- Allows specialization
- Clear separation of training goals
- Effective for advanced athletes

### Disadvantages
- Complex to design properly
- Long blocks with single focus can cause detraining
- Requires extensive training experience
- Less flexibility in schedule

## Concurrent Periodization (Mixed Model)

### Structure

Simultaneous development of multiple qualities with emphasis shifting weekly.

### Example: Concurrent Strength & Hypertrophy

**Week 1 (Strength Focus):**
- Compound lifts: 3x5 @ 85% (strength emphasis)
- Accessory: 3x10 @ 70% (hypertrophy maintenance)

**Week 2 (Hypertrophy Focus):**
- Compound lifts: 3x8 @ 70% (hypertrophy emphasis)
- Accessory: 4x12 @ 65% (hypertrophy focus)

**Week 3 (Balanced):**
- Compound lifts: 3x6 @ 75% (balanced)
- Accessory: 3x10 @ 70% (balanced)

### Advantages
- Develops multiple qualities simultaneously
- Flexible scheduling
- Can adjust emphasis based on recovery/goals

### Disadvantages
- Can dilute training effects
- More difficult to track progress
- Risk of overtraining if not careful

## Periodization Selection Decision Tree

```
Start
  ↓
Athlete Experience Level
  ↓
  ├─ Beginner → Linear Periodization
  ├─ Intermediate
  │   ↓
  │   Competition Timeline?
  │   ↓
  │   ├─ Clear single date → Linear or Undulating
  │   └─ Multiple or none → Undulating (DUP)
  └─ Advanced
      ↓
      Goals and Timeline
      ↓
      ├─ Single major competition → Block Periodization
      ├─ Multiple competitions → Undulating or Concurrent
      └─ No competitions → Undulating or Concurrent
```

## Deload Integration Across Models

**Linear:** Every 4th week deload (reduce volume 50%, maintain intensity)
**Undulating:** Every 4th week deload (all sessions at 60% normal intensity)
**Block:** 1 week deload between blocks (complete rest or very light activity)

**Deload Protocol:**
- Reduce training volume by 40-60%
- Maintain training intensity (keep heavy weight, fewer sets/reps)
- Keep exercise selection the same
- Focus on technique and movement quality
- Duration: 5-7 days

## Experience Level Guidelines

### Beginner (0-12 months)

**Recommended:** Linear periodization

**Rationale:**
- Simple and predictable
- Clear progression path
- Develops foundation across all qualities
- Easy to understand and execute

**Avoid:** Block periodization (too specialized)

### Intermediate (1-3 years)

**Recommended:** Linear or Undulating

**Rationale:**
- Can handle more complexity
- Benefits from variety in training
- Linear provides structure for goal tracking
- Undulating provides novel stimuli

**Best Starting Point:** Linear with some undulation elements

### Advanced (3+ years)

**Recommended:** Any model based on goals

**Rationale:**
- Can handle complex programming
- Benefits from specialization
- Understands training response well
- Can autoregulate effectively

**Best Model:** Depends on competition schedule and goals

## Periodization Programming Checklist

Before finalizing a periodized program:

- [ ] Model selection justified by athlete level
- [ ] Clear progression from phase to phase
- [ ] Deload weeks scheduled appropriately
- [ ] Volume and intensity inversely related (not both high simultaneously)
- [ ] Exercise selection appropriate for each phase
- [ ] Competition/peaking timing aligned
- [ ] Recovery adequately planned
- [ ] Progression criteria defined for each phase
- [ ] Transition between phases planned
- [ ] Exit criteria for each phase defined
