## Core Principle

Effective running training follows a **polarized distribution**: approximately 80% low-intensity and 20% high-intensity training, with minimal moderate-intensity ("junk miles") work.

## VDOT-Based Training Zones

### Zone Definitions

**VDOT** is a measure of running economy and aerobic capacity, calculable from recent race performances.

**Zone 1: Easy / Recovery (59-74% VO2max)**
- Purpose: Recovery, building aerobic base
- Talk test: Can hold full conversation easily
- Breathing: Nose breathing possible
- Typical use: Recovery runs, warm-up, cool-down
- Session duration: 20-60 minutes

**Zone 2: Marathon (75-82% VO2max)**
- Purpose: Endurance building, stamina
- Talk test: Can converse in short sentences
- Breathing: Controlled, rhythmic
- Typical use: Long runs, marathon-pace work
- Session duration: 60-180 minutes

**Zone 3: Threshold / Tempo (83-88% VO2max)**
- Purpose: Lactate threshold improvement
- Talk test: One-word answers only
- Breathing: Heavy but controlled
- Typical use: Tempo runs, cruise intervals
- Session duration: 20-60 minutes

**Zone 4: Interval / VO2max (89-94% VO2max)**
- Purpose: VO2max improvement, economy
- Talk test: Cannot talk
- Breathing: Very heavy
- Typical use: Track intervals, hill repeats
- Session duration: 15-40 minutes (excluding rest)

**Zone 5: Repetition / Speed (95-100% VO2max)**
- Purpose: Neuromuscular coordination, speed
- Talk test: Impossible
- Breathing: Maximal
- Typical use: Strides, short sprints, form drills
- Session duration: 10-20 minutes (excluding rest)

### Calculating Training Zones from VDOT

**Formula Reference:** Daniels' Running Formula

**Example VDOT 45:**
- Zone 1 Easy: 8:30-9:30 min/mile pace
- Zone 2 Marathon: 7:45-8:15 min/mile pace
- Zone 3 Tempo: 7:00-7:30 min/mile pace
- Zone 4 Interval: 6:15-6:45 min/mile pace
- Zone 5 Repetition: 5:30-6:00 min/mile pace

**Note:** Exact paces vary by individual VDOT score; use Daniels' VDOT calculator for precise zones.

## Polarized Training Distribution

### The 80/20 Rule

**Principle:** 80% of training at low intensity (Zones 1-2), 20% at high intensity (Zones 4-5)

**Rationale:**
- Low intensity builds aerobic base without excessive fatigue
- High intensity provides necessary stimulus for improvement
- Minimizing Zone 3 avoids "gray zone" training that fatigues without maximal benefit

**Weekly Distribution Example (5 runs/week):**

**Low Intensity (80%):**
- Monday: Easy run (Zone 1) - 8 km @ 9:00 min/km
- Wednesday: Easy run (Zone 1) - 8 km @ 9:00 min/km
- Friday: Long run (Zone 2) - 16 km @ 8:15 min/km
- Saturday: Recovery run (Zone 1) - 6 km @ 9:30 min/km

**High Intensity (20%):**
- Tuesday: Intervals (Zone 4) - 6x1km @ 6:30 min/km with 2 min rest

**Total:** 38 km weekly volume, polarized distribution

### Common Mistakes to Avoid

**Middle-Ground Training (Zone 3 trap):**
- Running everything "somewhat hard"
- Not easy enough for recovery, not hard enough for stimulus
- Result: Fatigue without adaptation

**Signs of Too Much Zone 3:**
- Stagnation in performance despite hard work
- Chronic fatigue, poor recovery
- Dread of training sessions
- Higher resting heart rate

**Correction:** Move easy runs easier (Zone 1), make hard runs harder (Zone 4-5)

## Training Components

### 1. Base Building (Foundation)

**Purpose:** Build aerobic capacity and running economy

**Guidelines:**
- Duration: 8-12 weeks
- Intensity: Primarily Zone 1-2
- Volume: Gradually increase by 10-15% weekly
- Frequency: 4-6 runs per week

**Typical Week:**
```
Monday: Rest or cross-training
Tuesday: Easy run 8 km (Zone 1)
Wednesday: Easy run 10 km (Zone 1)
Thursday: Rest or easy yoga
Friday: Easy run 8 km (Zone 1)
Saturday: Long run 15-20 km (Zone 2)
Sunday: Rest or active recovery
```

**Progression:**
- Week 1-4: Build to 30-40 km weekly volume
- Week 5-8: Build to 40-50 km weekly volume
- Week 9-12: Build to 50-60 km weekly volume

### 2. Tempo Work (Threshold Development)

**Purpose:** Improve lactate threshold and sustained speed

**Workout Types:**

**Steady-State Tempo:**
- Duration: 20-40 minutes continuous at Zone 3
- Example: 5 km @ 7:00 min/km (threshold pace)

**Cruise Intervals:**
- Structure: Multiple shorter Zone 3 segments with brief rest
- Example: 4x1.5 km @ 7:00 min/km with 1 min jog recovery

**Progression:**
- Week 1: 20 min continuous tempo
- Week 2: 25 min continuous tempo
- Week 3: 4x1 km cruise intervals
- Week 4: 30 min continuous tempo

### 3. Interval Training (VO2max Development)

**Purpose:** Improve VO2max, running economy, and race-specific fitness

**Workout Types:**

**Short Intervals (Zone 4):**
- Distance: 400m-1km repeats
- Pace: Zone 4 (3k-5k race pace)
- Rest: 2-3 minutes jog recovery
- Volume: 3-6 km total work

**Example:**
```
Warm-up: 2 km easy
Main: 6x800m @ 6:30 min/km with 400m jog recovery
Cool-down: 2 km easy
Total: ~8 km
```

**Hill Repeats:Zone 4-5)
- Duration: 30-90 second uphill repeats
- Grade: 5-8% incline
- Rest: Jog down recovery
- Volume: 8-12 repeats

**Progression:**
- Week 1: 4x400m intervals
- Week 2: 5x400m intervals
- Week 3: 6x600m intervals
- Week 4: 5x800m intervals

### 4. Tapering (Peaking for Competition)

**Purpose:** Reduce fatigue while maintaining fitness for peak performance

**Guidelines:**
- Duration: 7-14 days before major race
- Volume reduction: 40-60% of normal
- Intensity: Maintain (still include Zone 4-5 work)
- Pattern: Exponential decay (biggest reductions early)

**10-Day Taper Example (for 5k race):**
```
Day 10: Easy run 8 km (Zone 1)
Day 9: Intervals 3x400m @ Zone 4
Day 8: Easy run 6 km (Zone 1)
Day 7: Rest
Day 6: Tempo 3x1km @ Zone 3
Day 5: Easy run 5 km (Zone 1)
Day 4: Strides 4x100m
Day 3: Rest
Day 2: Easy run 3 km with strides
Day 1: Rest
Day 0: Race
```

## Sample Training Programs

### 5K Training Program (12 weeks)

**Base Phase (Weeks 1-4):**
- Monday: Rest
- Tuesday: Easy run 8 km
- Wednesday: Easy run 10 km
- Thursday: Rest
- Friday: Easy run 8 km
- Saturday: Long run 12-16 km
- Sunday: Rest

**Build Phase (Weeks 5-8):**
- Monday: Rest
- Tuesday: Intervals 4x400m @ Zone 4
- Wednesday: Easy run 8 km
- Thursday: Rest
- Friday: Tempo 20 min @ Zone 3
- Saturday: Long run 14-18 km
- Sunday: Rest

**Peak Phase (Weeks 9-12):**
- Monday: Rest
- Tuesday: Intervals 6x400m @ Zone 4
- Wednesday: Easy run 8 km
- Thursday: Rest
- Friday: Tempo 25 min @ Zone 3
- Saturday: Long run 12-16 km (tapering down)
- Sunday: Rest

**Deload:** Week 4 and Week 8 (reduce volume 40%)

### Half Marathon Training Program (16 weeks)

**Base Phase (Weeks 1-6):**
- Focus: Build to 40-50 km weekly volume
- Primary: Zone 1-2 running
- Long run: Progress to 18-21 km

**Build Phase (Weeks 7-12):**
- Focus: Add intensity while maintaining volume
- Tuesday: Intervals or hill repeats
- Thursday: Tempo or cruise intervals
- Saturday: Long run (21-24 km)
- Sunday: Recovery run or cross-training

**Peak Phase (Weeks 13-16):**
- Focus: Sharpen for race
- Maintain one quality workout per week
- Long run tapers from 21 km → 16 km → 12 km → 8 km
- Race week: Significant volume reduction

## Concurrent Training Considerations

### Strength Training for Runners

**Purpose:** Improve economy, prevent injury

**Guidelines:**
- Schedule: 2x/week (non-consecutive days)
- Timing: After easy runs or separate sessions
- Focus: Lower body strength, core stability

**Sample Runner Strength Session:**
```
Goblet Squats: 3x10
Romanian Deadlifts: 3x10
Lunges: 3x8 each leg
Calf Raises: 3x15
Planks: 3x45 seconds
Side Planks: 3x30 seconds each side
```

**Programming Note:**
- Keep weights moderate (Zone 1-2 effort)
- Avoid excessive soreness before quality runs
- Deload strength concurrently with running deloads

### Managing Interference Effect

**Principle:** Concurrent strength and endurance training can interfere with each other's adaptations.

**Mitigation Strategies:**
1. Separate sessions by 6+ hours when possible
2. Prioritize run quality before heavy strength sessions
3. Keep strength sessions focused and efficient
4. Don't max out on strength during peak running training
5. Periodize both modalities (strength emphasis in base, running emphasis in peak)

## Recovery for Runners

### Sleep Requirements
- Minimum: 7-9 hours per night
- Quality: Consistent bedtime, cool room, dark environment
- Naps: 20-30 minutes can help during high volume

### Nutrition Guidelines (Consult Registered Dietitian for specifics)
- Carbohydrate timing: Before and after long runs
- Protein timing: Post-run recovery (15-30g within 2 hours)
- Hydration: Monitor urine color, weight changes
- Iron: Monitor levels if fatigued (especially female runners)

### Active Recovery
- Easy Zone 1 runs (20-30 minutes)
- Walking, hiking, cycling
- Yoga, mobility work
- Swimming

## Training Load Management

### Acute:Chronic Workload Ratio

**Calculate:**
- Acute workload: Total training load past 7 days
- Chronic workload: Average training load past 28 days
- Ratio: Acute / Chronic

**Guidelines:**
- Safe: <1.2
- Moderate: 1.2-1.5
- High risk: >1.5

**Example:**
```
Acute (week 5): 45 km
Chronic (weeks 1-4 average): 35 km
Ratio: 45/35 = 1.29 (moderate risk zone)
```

**Action:** Be conservative with progression when ratio exceeds 1.3

## Common Running Injuries & Prevention

### Injury Risk Factors
- Rapid volume increases (>10% per week)
- Too much high-intensity running
- Inadequate recovery
- Weak stabilizer muscles
- Poor running mechanics

### Prevention Strategies
1. Follow 10% rule for volume increases
2. Adhere to polarized distribution
3. Include strength training 2x/week
4. Prioritize sleep and recovery
5. Listen to body (take rest days when needed)
6. Alternate running surfaces
7. Replace shoes every 500-800 km

### When to Seek Professional Help
- Persistent pain (>2 weeks) that doesn't improve with rest
- Pain that alters running form
- Swelling or visible deformity
- Loss of function or range of motion
- Numbness or tingling

## Running Training Checklist

Before finalizing a running program:

- [ ] VDOT-based zones calculated from recent performance
- [ ] Polarized distribution maintained (~80/20)
- [ ] Base building phase included for beginners
- [ ] Progressive overload in volume and intensity
- [ ] Deload weeks scheduled every 3-4 weeks
- [ ] Taper included before target races
- [ ] Recovery adequately prioritized
- [ ] Strength training integrated if appropriate
- [ ] Injury prevention strategies included
- [ ] Race-specific training in peak phase
